Source Content

High-Yield Summary

Definition & Epidemiology

  • Head trauma includes injuries to the scalp, skull and brain.
  • Trauma is the 4th leading cause of death in the source; incidence 2–3%.
  • Moderate–severe injuries constitute 30–40%.
  • Leading cause of death age 1–44; in men 44–64 it exceeds stroke.

Etiology

Motor vehicle accidents, falls, civil assaults, sports, missile, occupational and birth injuries.

Types

Scalp: contusions, abrasions, lacerations, avulsion, hematomas. Skull: linear, depressed, diastatic, growing, skull base, maxillofacial. TBI: concussion, DAI, contusions (coup/countercoup/intermediate/gliding/herniation), edema, traumatic hemorrhages, cranial nerve injuries, traumatic vascular injuries.

Open / Closed

Open = scalp violated. Penetrating = dura breached + brain penetrated; non-penetrating = dura intact. Closed = scalp intact. Secondary injuries listed: brain edema and infarct.

General Management

Prevention: helmets/seat belts, combat impaired driving, anticipate secondary injury. ABC: patent airway → ventilation/O₂ → circulation. Two major IV lines, Foley, fluids, compatible blood transfusion, antishock measures.

Drugs / ICP

Antitetanic and anti-gas gangrene sera, antibiotics, analgesics, inotropes; steroids contraindicated. Antiepileptics: levetiracetam and diphenyl hydantoin. ICP/edema: mannitol, hypertonic saline, frusemide; elevate head.

GCS

Eye 4–1, Verbal 5–1, Motor 6–1. Minor 13–15, Moderate 9–12, Severe 3–8.

Surgery

Mass lesion indications: extradural, acute subdural, intracerebral with mass effect, contusion with midline shift, severe edema with herniation/midline shift → craniotomy/evacuation or decompressive craniectomy + duroplasty. IVH/post-traumatic hydrocephalus → external ventricular CSF drainage.

Skull Fractures

Linear = most common, no displacement, no surgery. Depressed = sunken-in segment, may need surgery. Basal = most serious; CSF fistula and cranial nerve injury possible.

Basal Skull Fractures by Site

Anterior: epistaxis, subconjunctival hemorrhage, raccoon eyes, CSF rhinorrhea, olfactory injury. Middle: bleeding per ear, CSF otorrhea, VII/VIII injury. Posterior: Battle's sign, lower cranial nerve injury, brain-stem injury, atlantoaxial subluxation.

Herniation

Subfalcine: no clinical effect. Central tentorial: worsening consciousness, DI, pupils initially small then fixed/dilated. Tonsillar: worsening consciousness + Cushing triad (hypertension, bradycardia, Cheyne-Stokes breathing).

Imaging

Plain X-ray: fractures, shifted pineal may indicate side of mass. CT: acute traumatic findings including hematomas, hemorrhage, contusion, edema, foreign body, infarct, hydrocephalus, mass effect/midline/brain shift. MRI: chronic extra-axial hematomas, contusions, DAI/shear injury, empyema. Angiography: dissection, false aneurysm, vascular occlusion.

Concussion

Temporary loss of brain function; may follow blow or acceleration-deceleration without direct impact; most common TBI; monitoring and rest; confusion/amnesia usually subside within 3 weeks though may persist.
